## Why This Source Matters

Jamar Diggs is a strong source for this project because he focuses on YouTube as a business and marketing tool, not just a creator platform.

His approach is especially relevant to B2B SaaS because many companies misunderstand YouTube. They think they need to become influencers, post constantly, or chase subscribers. Jamar’s approach focuses more on business outcomes, organic discovery, and attracting the right audience.

For this research project, Jamar Diggs helps explain how companies can use YouTube to attract qualified leads, build authority, and support long-term business growth.

## Key Takeaways

- Businesses should not treat YouTube like a creator/influencer platform.
- YouTube can work as a long-term organic marketing channel.
- The goal should not only be subscribers or views; the goal should be attracting the right potential customers.
- Simple, consistent, helpful content can be more useful than complex production.
- YouTube content should be connected to the company’s business goals.
- Businesses should create videos that move viewers into the broader marketing ecosystem.
- A strong YouTube strategy should help problem-aware viewers understand why the company or expert is credible.

## Notes

The main lesson from this source is that companies should approach YouTube with business logic, not creator logic.

For B2B SaaS companies, this is important because the goal is not entertainment or ad revenue. The goal is to attract the right buyers, educate them, and help them move closer to a sales conversation.

A SaaS company does not need to publish like a full-time YouTuber. Instead, it can create focused videos that answer buyer questions, explain problems, and help viewers understand the company’s point of view.

Useful B2B SaaS YouTube topics could include:
- Why a certain problem is costing teams money
- Mistakes companies make before buying software
- How to compare different solution types
- What buyers should know before choosing a platform
- How successful teams solve a specific workflow problem
- What to ask before booking a demo
- How to calculate the cost of doing nothing

This source also reinforces the idea that YouTube should not stop at the video. The channel should connect viewers to the next step, such as a newsletter, lead magnet, demo page, webinar, or sales resource.

## B2B SaaS Insight

For B2B SaaS companies, YouTube should be designed as a lead-generation and trust-building system.

The channel should help the right people:
- Discover the company
- Understand the problem
- Trust the company’s expertise
- Learn the company’s approach
- Move into the marketing ecosystem
- Become more qualified before sales

This connects directly to B2B SaaS because buyers often need education before they are ready to speak with sales.

Jamar Diggs’ approach suggests that SaaS companies should stop asking, “How do we become popular on YouTube?”

Instead, they should ask:
- “Who are we trying to attract?”
- “What problem are they already aware of?”
- “What content would make them trust us?”
- “What should they do after watching?”
- “How does this video support the sales journey?”

## Possible Playbook Rules

1. Treat YouTube as a business growth channel, not an influencer platform.
2. Create videos for qualified buyers, not the general internet.
3. Prioritize useful, simple, consistent content over overproduction.
4. Connect every video to a business goal.
5. Move viewers into a wider marketing ecosystem after watching.
6. Focus on problem-aware viewers who are more likely to become leads.
7. Use YouTube to build authority and trust before the sales conversation.
8. Measure success by qualified leads, buyer trust, and business relevance — not views alone.
