# Chris Walker – Demand Generation, Dark Social, and Company POV

## Expert

Chris Walker

## Source

- Title: Chris Walker, CEO, Refine Labs
- Type: Podcast episode / interview
- Source link: https://www.exitfive.com/podcast/chris-walker-ceo-refine-labs
- Date collected: 2026-04-24

## Why This Source Matters

Chris Walker is a strong source for this project because he is closely associated with modern B2B demand generation, dark social, self-reported attribution, and challenging traditional lead-generation models.

This source is useful because YouTube strategy for B2B SaaS is not only about publishing videos. It is also about understanding how modern buyers discover, trust, and evaluate companies before they ever book a demo.

For this research project, Chris Walker helps explain why content channels like YouTube, LinkedIn, podcasts, and community should be treated as demand creation channels, not only as direct-response lead capture tools.

## Key Takeaways

- B2B buyers often discover companies through channels that are hard to track directly.
- Traditional attribution tools may undercount the impact of content, social, YouTube, podcasting, and word-of-mouth.
- Strong B2B marketing needs a clear company point of view, not just product promotion.
- Demand generation is different from lead generation; the goal is to create trust and buyer readiness before capture.
- Self-reported attribution can help companies understand what influenced buyers when software tracking is incomplete.
- LinkedIn, community, podcasts, and video can influence demand even when they do not create immediate form fills.
- B2B content should educate the market and shape how buyers think, not only push conversion offers.

## Notes

The main lesson from this source is that B2B SaaS content should be judged by how it creates demand, not only by how it captures leads.

This applies directly to YouTube. A SaaS company may publish videos that do not immediately create demo requests, but those videos can still influence future pipeline by educating buyers, increasing trust, and making the company more familiar.

Chris Walker’s view is useful because it challenges the idea that every marketing activity must be measured only through last-click attribution.

For YouTube, this means a video strategy should consider:
- How videos change buyer beliefs
- How videos create category awareness
- How videos build trust before a demo
- How videos support word-of-mouth
- How videos give sales teams better conversations
- How videos make the company’s point of view more visible

This is especially relevant in B2B SaaS because buyers often research privately before contacting vendors. They may watch YouTube videos, read LinkedIn posts, listen to podcasts, ask peers, and visit communities before filling out a form.

If the company only measures what attribution software can see, it may undervalue YouTube and other trust-building channels.

## B2B SaaS Insight

For B2B SaaS companies, YouTube should be treated as a demand creation channel.

The role of YouTube is not only to drive direct conversions. It can also:
- Teach the market
- Build category trust
- Make the company’s POV visible
- Support dark social and word-of-mouth
- Help buyers understand the problem
- Increase familiarity before sales conversations

This connects Chris Walker’s demand generation thinking to YouTube strategy.

A strong B2B SaaS YouTube channel should not be judged only by views, subscribers, or direct demo conversions. It should also be judged by whether it helps create more informed, more trusting, and more qualified buyers.

## Possible Playbook Rules

1. Treat YouTube as demand creation, not only lead capture.
2. Build videos around a clear company point of view.
3. Use content to educate the market before buyers enter the pipeline.
4. Do not rely only on last-click attribution to judge video impact.
5. Ask buyers how they discovered and trusted the company.
6. Use YouTube, LinkedIn, podcasts, and community together as influence channels.
7. Measure content by trust, buyer quality, pipeline influence, and sales conversations.
8. Create content that changes buyer beliefs, not just content that asks for demos.
